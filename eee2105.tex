\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, title = \textbf{Q1. }Why we use semi-conductors for making devices?]
    We use semiconductors for making devices because they allow precise control over the flow of electric current, which is essential for modern electronics. In metals, the electrical conductivity is always high and cannot be easily altered, so they are not suitable for switching or controlling signals. Semiconductors, on the other hand, have conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator, and this property can be modified by adding impurities (doping) or by applying external voltages, light, or temperature changes. This controllability makes them perfect for building components like diodes, transistors, and integrated circuits. By adjusting their conductivity, we can create devices that act as switches, amplifiers, or sensors. Semiconductors also allow the miniaturization of circuits, enabling the creation of compact, high-speed, and low-power devices. Without semiconductors, technologies such as computers, smartphones, and solar cells would not be possible. Essentially, semiconductors provide the foundation for controlling and processing electrical signals efficiently in almost all electronic systems.
\end{tcolorbox}
