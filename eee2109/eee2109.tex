\section{Introduction}
\textbf{Signal : }Signal is a function of one or more independent variables that
carries information (usually through amplitude, phase,
frequency and position etc.) about the behavior of the
physical variable/phenomenon.
\[
F(x_1,x_2,x_3,\cdots\cdots,x_n)
\]
Here,\par
 F $\longrightarrow$ Signal\par
$x_1,x_2,x_3,\cdots, x_n \longrightarrow$ independent variable\par
\begin{itemize}
    \item Anything which is constant is not a signal.
    \item AC$\rightarrow$Signal,DC $\rightarrow$ Not a signal.
    
\end{itemize}
\textbf{\large Types of Signal :}\par
There are two types of signal.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item [(i)]Single variable signal : Depends on one independent variable.\\
\underline{Example :} $f(x),g(t)$
\item[(ii)] Multi-variable signal : Depends on more than one independent variable.\\
\underline{Example :} $f(x_1,x_2),g(t_1,t_2,t_3)$

\end{enumerate}
\textbf{System :} The meaningfull interaction of physical devices and components is called as system.
\begin{itemize}
    \item System alone can't achieve anything,it must link with signal.
    $$ i/p \longrightarrow \boxed{\text{System}}\longrightarrow o/p $$
    \item i/p signal $\longrightarrow f(x_1,x_2) $\\
    \item o/p signal $\longrightarrow g(x_1,x_2) $
     Both are same independent variable.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scaling of CTS}
There are two types of scaling in CT signal.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Time scaling.
    \item Amplitude scaling.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{1.Time scaling :} The compression or expansion of a signal in time.\par
\[ x(t) \xrightarrow{\text{T.Sc}} y(t)=x(\alpha t) \]
\textbf{Case-1}
\[
|\alpha| > 1 \longrightarrow \alpha\in (-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)
\]

%graph
This is the case of compression.\\
\textbf{Case-2}
\[
|\alpha|<1 \longrightarrow \alpha \neq 0 \quad \quad[\alpha=-0.3,-0.2,-0.1,-0.099 \cdots\cdots 0.1,0.2,0.3]
\]

%graph

This is the case of expansion.\\
\underline{Shortcut} 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Amplitude remain same.
    \item Times are divided by $\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{2.Amplitude Scaling :} The increased and decreased of a signal in time.
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow{A.Sc} y(t)=\beta x(t)
\]
\textbf{Case-1}
\[
|\beta | >1 \longrightarrow \beta \in(-\infty,-1]\cup [1,\infty)
\]

%graph

Thsi is the case of amplitude expansion.\par
\textbf{Case-2}
\[
|\beta|<1 \longrightarrow \beta \in (-1,0)\cup (0,1)
\]

%graph

This is the case of amplitude reduction.
\section{Shifting of CTS}
There are two types of shifting in CT signal.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Time shifting.
    \item Amplitude shifting.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{1.Time Shifting : }Time shifting means shifting a signal in time, either forward (delay) or backward (advance), without changing its shape.
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow{T.Sf} y(t)=x(t+K)
\]
\textbf{Case-1} $k>0$\\
%graph
This is the case of left shifting or time advanced.\\
\textbf{Case-2 } $K<0$\\
%graph
This is the case of right shifting or time delay.\\
\textcolor{red}{Advancing time signal is not possible in real life.}\\
\textbf{2.Amplitude Shifting :} Amplitude shifting means adding or subtracting a constant value to the signal’s amplitude, which shifts it vertically up or down on the graph.\\
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow{A.Sf} y(t)=x(t)+l
\]
\textbf{Case-1} $l>0$\\
%graph
This is the case of upward shifting.\\
\textbf{Case-2} $l<0$\\
%graph
This is the case of downward shifting.\\
\section{Folding/Reversal of CTS }
\textbf{1.Time Reversal :} Time reversal means flipping a signal across the vertical axis (t = 0 line).It inverse signal in time.\\
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow{T.Sc} y(t)=x(\alpha t)
\]
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow[\alpha=-1]{T.R} y(t)=x(-t)
\]
%graph
This is the special case of time scaling with $\alpha=-1$.It is also called as folding or reflection.\\
\textbf{2.Amplitude Reversal :} Amplitude reversal means flipping a signal across the horizontal axis (amplitude axis).
It changes the sign of the amplitude values.
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow{A.Sc} y(t)=\beta x(t)
\]
\[
x(t)\xrightarrow[\beta=-1]{A.R} y(t)= -x(t)
\]
This is the special case of amplitude scaling with $\beta=-1$.It is fold signal with respect to Y-axis.
